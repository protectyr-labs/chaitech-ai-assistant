# /brand-values

Optional exercise. Brian Hickling's "Brand Values as a Product" 4-step process from his Catalyst 17 work. Use after the core 8 exercises, especially if the brand intends to lead with values (purpose-driven, mission-driven, social-impact, B-Corp-style).

## Pre-checks

- `brand-workshop/STATE.md` must exist.
- Sections 1–8 should be filled. Values without purpose underneath are decoration; insist on running the core first.

## Frame (one sentence)

> "Brian Hickling's argument is that brand values are a *product* — they have to be testable, observable, and useful, not just aspirational adjectives on a wall. Four steps."

## The four steps

Walk through them in order.

### Step 1 — List the candidate values

> "Start wide. What words describe how your brand *behaves*? Not what it makes — how it acts. We want 8–15 candidates. Use the bolded words from your Purpose Words exercise (section 5) as a head start."

Capture as a flat list.

### Step 2 — Test each candidate against three filters

For each candidate value, ask:

1. **Is it true?** — Can you point to a moment in the last 90 days where this value showed up in your behavior? (If pre-launch, your behavior in past work.)
2. **Is it differentiating?** — Would your nearest competitor list this same value? If yes, it might be table stakes, not a value.
3. **Is it actionable?** — Could a new hire on day one tell whether they were violating it?

A value that fails any of the three goes on a **shortlist for revision**, not the final list.

### Step 3 — Cut to 3–5

> "Brand values lose force above five. Your job now is to cut. Which 3 to 5 *must* survive?"

Force a ranking. Ties get broken by: which one is most expensive to live by? That one stays.

### Step 4 — Make each value testable

For each surviving value, write three artifacts:

```markdown
**<Value name>**
- *Definition:* <one sentence in plain language>
- *Behavior we expect:* <a specific example of this value in action>
- *Behavior we will not tolerate:* <a specific example of violating it>
```

The "will not tolerate" line is the most important. A value with no anti-pattern is decoration.

## Constraints

- Do not allow more than 5 final values. Above 5, value force decays.
- Do not allow values without anti-patterns. The "will not tolerate" line is non-optional.
- If the founder picks "Integrity" or "Excellence" or "Innovation" — ask once: "What is your specific phrasing of that, in your voice? Other brands say those words too."
- Cite Brian Hickling as the methodology source in the section header.
