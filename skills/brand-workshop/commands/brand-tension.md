# /brand-tension

Captures section 12 of `STATE.md` — the central tension the brand has to navigate, expressed as two endpoints with a chosen position between them.

## Pre-checks

- `brand-workshop/STATE.md` must exist.
- Sections 1, 2, 4, 7, 11 should be filled (stakeholders, needs, purpose, superpower, IS/IS NOT). Tension is named most accurately *after* those.

## Frame (one paragraph)

> "Every strong brand sits in productive tension. Not contradiction — tension. Examples: optimism vs. urgency. Growth vs. mission integrity. Disruption vs. trust. Innovation vs. reliability. Specialist vs. generalist. We're going to name yours and decide where you sit on it."

## The conversation

1. Ask:

   > "What two opposing forces does your brand have to balance every day? What's the choice you keep making — and the choice you keep refusing to make purely?"

2. If the founder struggles, offer a heuristic:

   > "Read your IS / IS NOT list (section 11). Often the central tension hides between two adjacent items there. Look at brands you admire — Apple sits between 'simplicity' and 'sophistication'. Patagonia sits between 'commerce' and 'mission integrity'. What's yours?"

3. Once they have a candidate, ask:

   > "Name the left endpoint in 1–3 words. Name the right endpoint in 1–3 words."

4. Then ask the position question:

   > "On a scale of 0 to 100, where do you sit between those two endpoints? 50 means perfect middle. Below 50 means you lean left; above 50 means you lean right."

5. Then the rationale:

   > "Briefly — what does that tension capture for you? When does it show up most? Where does it resolve?"

## Test the tension

Before writing, run a single sanity check:

> "If a customer or hire only knew this one tension and where you sit on it, would they make better day-to-day decisions about your brand than they would without it? If yes, we have a useful tension. If not, it's too abstract — let's pick a sharper one."

## Write

```markdown
## 12. Central Brand Tension

**Endpoints:**  <left endpoint, 1–3 words>  ←→  <right endpoint, 1–3 words>

**Position:** <0–100>  *(0 = full left, 100 = full right, 50 = perfect middle)*

**Why this tension, and why this position:**
<2–4 sentences. Specific. The "tension capture" — when does this tension show up?
What pulls you left? What pulls you right? Why have you landed where you have?>
```

## Wrap

> "Written to section 12. The central tension is one of the most useful artifacts when you're hiring, briefing a designer, or deciding whether to take an opportunity that doesn't quite fit. Re-read it when a decision feels off. Next: `/brand-not-doing` — the explicit list of things your brand will NOT do."

## Constraints

- Refuse abstract tensions ("good vs. bad"). Push for specific, ownable framings.
- A tension with both endpoints in the same family of values isn't a tension. "Quality vs. excellence" is not productive. "Speed vs. craft" might be.
- The position number matters. If the founder picks "50 — right in the middle", probe: "Truly? Or are you avoiding the trade-off?" A genuine 50 is rare; most brands lean.
- Do not force a tension if the founder genuinely doesn't have one yet. Capture "(too early to tell — revisit at 6 months)" honestly. A premature tension is worse than no tension.
