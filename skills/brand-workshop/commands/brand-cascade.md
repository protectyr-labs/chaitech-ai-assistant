# /brand-cascade

Exercise 7. Five interlocking choices from Lafley & Martin's *Playing to Win*. The cascade is what turns brand purpose into a workable strategy.

## Pre-checks

- `brand-workshop/STATE.md` must exist.
- Sections 1–6 should be filled. The cascade depends on them; warn if any are missing but allow override.

## Frame the exercise (once)

> "This is borrowed from a strategy book called *Playing to Win* by Roger Martin and A.G. Lafley, the team that doubled Procter & Gamble's revenue in the 2000s. The idea: strategy is a set of five interlocking choices. Skip one and the others fall apart. We will fill them top to bottom — and then check that they hold together."

## The five cells

Walk through them in order. **One cell at a time.** Each cell ends with a write-back to `brand-workshop/STATE.md`.

### Cell 1 — Winning Aspiration

> "What does *winning* look like for your brand, three to five years out? Not a revenue target — the position. *Become the default X for Y.* *Be the brand that Z is named after.*"

Push for ambition that is specific, not generic. "Be #1 in the market" is not an aspiration; "be the brand a small Canadian credit union calls first when their cyber insurance asks for an assessment" is.

### Cell 2 — Where to Play

> "Where do you compete, and just as important, where do you *not*? Cover five angles: who you sell to, what geography, what channels, what offers / categories, what kind of buyer."

Force the negative space. Ask: "Name one customer segment you will deliberately *not* serve, and why." A cascade with no exclusions is fantasy.

### Cell 3 — How to Win

> "Inside the playing field you just defined, what is your sustainable unfair advantage? Three to five reasons buyers in *that* segment pick *you* over the alternatives."

Anchor in the superpower (section 6) and the long-form purpose (section 4). The "how to win" should be a logical extension of those, not a fresh invention.

### Cell 4 — Capabilities

> "What must you actually be able to *do* — as a team, as a system — to win that way? List your existing strengths AND the gaps you have to close."

Force the gap list. A capability section that only lists strengths is a brochure, not a strategy.

### Cell 5 — Management Systems

> "And what systems / habits / measurements will keep those capabilities sharp? Tools, rituals, hires, dashboards, weekly cadences, financial controls."

If the founder is solo and pre-systems, reframe: "What three systems would you put in place *first* — given the aspiration above — if you had a free Tuesday?"

## Coherence check

After all five are written, read them top to bottom and ask the founder one question:

> "Does cell 4 actually deliver cell 3? Does cell 5 actually keep cell 4 sharp? If any link breaks, we go back."

The cascade only works if the choices reinforce each other. If a link breaks, redo that cell.

> "Written to section 7. Last core exercise is `/brand-pillars` — the 3–4 things you must keep doing to live the purpose. After that, optional layers (`/brand-values`, `/brand-movement`) and `/brand-export` to render the deck."

## Constraints

- Do not let the founder fill all five cells in one breath. Each cell needs its own conversation, even if short.
- The "Where to play" cell must include an exclusion. If the founder cannot name one, the cascade is incoherent — keep digging.
- The "Capabilities" cell must include gaps. If the founder names only strengths, ask: "What capability would you have to *develop* to win in cell 3?"
- Do not silently substitute "playing to win" terminology if the founder uses different words. Capture their language; gloss the term once and move on.
