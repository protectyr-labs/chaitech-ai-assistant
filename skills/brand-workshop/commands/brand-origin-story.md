# /brand-origin-story

Captures section 9 of `STATE.md` — the founder's origin story. Brands live through stories; the origin is the most reliable one.

## Pre-checks

- `brand-workshop/STATE.md` must exist.
- This exercise can be run any time; it does not depend on other sections. If the founder has not done sections 1–4 yet, suggest doing this one first — origin often clarifies who the brand serves and why.

## Frame (one paragraph)

> "Every strong brand traces back to a moment. Not a market analysis, not a TAM slide — a frustration, an insight, a thing somebody close to you couldn't get and you decided to fix. That's the origin. Customers, hires, and investors remember origins. They don't remember mission statements."

## The conversation

1. Ask one open question:

   > "Tell me the moment, the frustration, or the insight that made you start this. Tell it the way you'd tell it to a friend over a beer — not the polished version."

2. **Listen first. Do not edit.** Capture what they said. If it's two sentences, that's fine. If it's six paragraphs, that's also fine.

3. Once they've answered, ask **one** sharpening question (pick whichever fits):
   - "Was there a specific person you were thinking of when you started? Who?"
   - "What was the moment you knew you had to build this and not just complain about it?"
   - "If you had to point to one customer or one situation that crystallized why this matters, what is it?"

4. Reflect their origin back in one tight paragraph and ask: "Does that sound like you?"

5. Iterate until they say yes.

## Write

```markdown
## 9. Origin Story

<200 words max, in the founder's voice. The moment, the frustration, the insight.
A real person in it where possible. Not polished.>
```

## Wrap

> "Written to section 9. The origin story is one of the highest-leverage assets of the workbook — designers, hires, investors, and customers all use it. Bring it back if your purpose statement (section 4) needs sharpening."

Suggest the next command based on what's done. If they came here right after `/brand-start`, suggest `/brand-stakeholders` next.

## Constraints

- **Refuse the polished version.** If the founder gives a corporate, third-person, "we identified a market gap" answer, push back: "Tell me the actual moment, not the press release version. What were you doing? Who were you angry at?"
- Keep their voice intact. Do not tighten or "improve" the prose. The roughness is the point.
- 200 words is a soft cap. If they need 300, that's fine. The word count exists to keep them from over-writing, not to cut a real story short.
- If the founder genuinely doesn't have one ("we just saw a market"), capture that honestly. A weak origin is data — flag it gently as something worth revisiting.
