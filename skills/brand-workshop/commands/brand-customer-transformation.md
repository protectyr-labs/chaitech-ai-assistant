# /brand-customer-transformation

Captures section 10 of `STATE.md` — the specific change in the customer's life, work, or business after engaging with the brand. "From X to Y" is how a brand promise becomes concrete.

## Pre-checks

- `brand-workshop/STATE.md` must exist.
- Sections 1–2 (stakeholders + needs) should be filled. Without them, "transformation" floats. Allow override but warn.

## Frame (one sentence)

> "Most brands describe themselves by what they make. Strong brands describe themselves by what changes for the customer. We're going to draft that change."

## The conversation

1. Ask:

   > "Pick your stakeholder #1 from section 1. Six months after they engaged with you, what's different about their life, work, or business? Specificity wins. 'Better outcomes' is invisible. 'They went from doing X every Tuesday to doing Y instead' is the brand."

2. If they answer with abstractions ("they're more efficient", "they have peace of mind"), push:

   > "What does that look like on a Tuesday? What did they used to spend an hour doing that they now spend 10 minutes on? What conversation did they used to dread that's now easy?"

3. Encourage the **before / after structure**:

   > "Try this shape: Before us, they were [X]. Six months in, they are [Y]. The unlock was [Z]."

4. After stakeholder #1, optionally repeat for stakeholder #2 if their transformation is meaningfully different. Stop at three at most.

5. Reflect back. Land on one paragraph the founder agrees with.

## Write

```markdown
## 10. Customer Transformation

### Stakeholder 1: <name>
**Before:** <one sentence>
**After (6 months in):** <one sentence>
**The unlock:** <one sentence>

### Stakeholder 2: <name> (optional)
...
```

## Wrap

> "Written to section 10. This is one of the strongest assets you have for marketing copy and case studies — pull from this section verbatim when you write a homepage or a customer story. Suggest running `/brand-tension` next — defining the productive tension your brand navigates pairs well with the transformation you just named."

## Constraints

- Refuse "they have better outcomes." Always push to a Tuesday-morning specific.
- If the founder is pre-launch and has zero customers, reframe: "Imagine the customer you most want to serve. Six months in — what specifically would change for them?" Mark the section as `(hypothesized — pre-launch)` so it's clear this is intent, not evidence.
- Do not write the founder's transformation for them. You can offer 2–3 alternative phrasings, but the words must be theirs.
- One stakeholder is enough. Don't insist on three if the first one is strong.
