# /brand-movement

Optional exercise. Brian Hickling's argument: in 2026, a purpose alone is not enough — purpose-driven brands need a *movement* to activate the purpose into something customers join, not just buy.

## Pre-checks

- `brand-workshop/STATE.md` must exist.
- Sections 4 (purpose), 6 (superpower), and 8 (pillars) should all be filled. Movement work is meaningless without those.

## Frame (one paragraph)

> "Brian Hickling's data: 76% of brands say they have a purpose; only 10% have an activation plan behind it. The gap is the difference between *having* a purpose and *running* a movement around it. A movement has a name, an enemy, a rallying cry, a participation pattern, and a measurement. We will draft those five."

## The five elements

### 1. The name

> "What do we call this thing? Not the brand name — the *movement* name. *Get out the vote. Black Lives Matter. The Slow Food Movement. The Right to Repair.* Two to four words. Memorable. Carries a verb or a noun with weight."

Push past corporate phrasing. The name has to feel like something a stranger could put on a sign.

### 2. The enemy

> "What is the movement *against*? Pick the specific status quo, system, or behavior you exist to displace. Without an enemy, a movement is a hobby."

Force specificity. "Bad coffee" is too soft; "industrial-grade espresso served by people who do not care" is right.

### 3. The rallying cry

> "One sentence — short, strong, repeatable — that captures what someone says when they join. Not a tagline (tagline is for the brand). The rallying cry is for the cause."

Examples to offer:
- *Make security defensible. Not theoretical.*
- *Coffee should taste like the place it came from.*
- *No founder has to figure brand out alone.*

### 4. The participation pattern

> "How does someone *join* the movement? What is the smallest, lowest-friction thing they can do today to be part of it?"

Movements need entry points. Buying the product is one — but think wider. Sharing a piece of content. Posting a sticker. Using a hashtag. Showing up at a meetup. Adopting a practice.

### 5. The measurement

> "How do we know the movement is growing? Not revenue — *adoption*. What number do we watch?"

Examples:
- Customers who refer a friend
- People using a hashtag
- Brands that adopt the same standard
- Press mentions of the movement (not the brand)

## Constraints

- A movement without an enemy is a hobby. If the founder will not name one, the exercise is incomplete — push gently.
- The rallying cry must be a sentence, not a slogan. "Be more, do more" is a slogan. "No founder has to figure brand out alone" is a rallying cry.
- The measurement must NOT be revenue. Movement growth is upstream of revenue; measuring it as revenue defeats the exercise.
- Cite Brian Hickling and Catalyst 17 in the section header.
