Brand DNA · Filled Example (Fictional)

Lumen Coffee Roasters

What a finished workshop looks like

Owner: Maria Costa, founder & head roaster Location: Burlington, Ontario Status: v1 — exported from brand-workshop
This is a fictional example. Lumen Coffee Roasters does not exist. The brand was made up to show what a fully filled Brand Workshop output looks like end-to-end. Use it to calibrate the level of detail and specificity to aim for in your own workshop.

1 · Stakeholders

Everyone the brand serves; the three most vital.
All stakeholders we considered:
  • Walk-in regulars (the morning rush — same faces, same orders)
  • Weekend tourists from Toronto and Hamilton
  • Wholesale cafe accounts (12 cafes across the GTA)
  • Restaurant accounts (3 mid-range restaurants)
  • Subscription bag customers (online direct-to-home)
  • Origin farm partners (Honduras, Ethiopia, Colombia)
  • Importers (one main, one backup)
  • Roastery staff (5 baristas, 2 roasters, 1 roastery manager)
  • Maria's family (small business co-financed by spouse)
  • Landlord (one-shop lease tied to neighbourhood viability)
  • Local press & food bloggers
  • City of Burlington (municipal small-business grants, zoning)
  • Equipment vendors (espresso machine, grinder, roaster)
  • Specialty Coffee Association (industry recognition / standards)

Stakeholder 1

The Daily Regular
A 32-year-old who works from home three days a week, lives within walking distance, and treats the morning visit as the only fixed point in their day.

Stakeholder 2

The Cafe-Owner Wholesaler
A small independent cafe owner in the GTA who buys 20–60 lbs/week, needs reliable delivery, and uses Lumen's name on their menu as a credibility marker.

Stakeholder 3

The Origin Farm Partner
A second-generation farmer in Marcala, Honduras who sells 80% of their crop through specialty importers and depends on multi-year price commitments to plan reinvestment.

3 · Purpose Archetype

How Lumen makes the world a slightly better place.

Encourage Exploration — Lumen exists to take people to a new tasting experience every season. Single-origin rotation, farm stories, brewing classes, tasting flights. The brand's job is to make discovery feel like a gentle adventure rather than a snobbery test.

Foster Prosperity (secondary) — long-term price commitments and pre-financing for origin farms turn the supply chain into a partnership rather than an extraction. Wholesale cafe accounts grow alongside Lumen.

Why this archetype, not "Kindle Happiness": happiness is the side effect, not the purpose. Anyone can sell joy in a paper cup; Lumen earns its place by guiding people somewhere they would not have gone alone. The November visit when Maria poured a Marcala honey-process for a regular who said "I never knew coffee could taste like that" — that moment is the brand.

7 · Our Superpower

What the world needs most

Coffee that is local, traceable, and tastes like the place it came from — without the snobbery wall that keeps most drinkers from ever trying it.

What we are uniquely gifted at

Roasting profiles built around the bean, not the equipment. Maria's eight years cupping at Q-grader level. Direct relationships with three origins, no broker layer. A teaching style that makes a beginner feel smart in 90 seconds.

What we are passionate about

The look on a regular's face when an unfamiliar single-origin lands and they say "wait — what is this?" That moment is why the doors open every morning.

Intersection: Lumen is the bridge between curious drinkers who deserve better and origin farms whose craft is invisible — using direct sourcing, seasonal rotation, and patient teaching to make discovery feel ordinary.

8 · Strategic Cascade (Roger Martin · Playing to Win)

Winning Aspiration

Become the specialty roaster of record for greater Hamilton / Burlington — the brand the next wave of indie cafes name on their menus and the brand Toronto coffee tourists drive 45 minutes to visit on a weekend.

Where to Play

  • Buyers: walk-in regulars; indie cafes & small restaurants in GTA; subscription bag customers in Ontario.
  • Geo: Hamilton/Burlington beachhead. GTA wholesale. Online Ontario only (no cross-border yet).
  • Channels: single retail location; wholesale direct sales; e-commerce subscription.
  • Will not play: grocery shelf, Amazon, drive-through, white-label private brands. Those are volume games; Lumen is a discovery game.

How to Win

  • Direct origin relationships — three farms by name, multi-year contracts.
  • Seasonal rotation — a new single-origin every 90 days, brewed and taught.
  • Teaching as marketing — free Saturday brewing class earns more goodwill than any ad.
  • Cafe partnerships that elevate — train wholesale buyers, don't just deliver beans.

Capabilities

  • Q-grader-level cupping (Maria).
  • Direct importer relationship secured for two of three origins.
  • Roastery production capacity 200 lbs/week, headroom to 350.
  • Wholesale CRM & delivery routing.
  • Gaps: e-commerce subscription tech is duct-taped; need a real platform. No content engine for stories. No second roaster trained.

Management Systems

  • Weekly cupping discipline — every roast tasted before it ships.
  • Monthly wholesale partner check-in (10 min each).
  • Quarterly origin trip or call (one of three farms each quarter).
  • Saturday brewing class as fixed ritual — never cancelled.
  • Inventory + COGS tracker per origin to defend pricing.

9 · Strategic Pillars

01

Direct Origin

Every coffee on the menu has a farm name and a story. No anonymous blends, no broker chains. If we cannot name the origin, we do not roast it.

02

Seasonal Rotation

The single-origin slot rotates every 90 days. Customers expect change. The menu is a calendar, not a catalog.

03

Teach the Cup

Saturday brewing class, never cancelled. Wholesale partners trained, not just supplied. A regular should know more about coffee after a year with us than before.

04

Generous Hospitality

Free water, free Wi-Fi, dogs on the patio, no rush at the till. Hospitality is a margin line we are willing to lose to keep.

10 · Values (Brian Hickling · 4-step values process)

Patience
Definition: We let the bean tell us when it is ready, not the schedule.
Behavior we expect: Pull a roast from the schedule if a green sample isn't right.
Will not tolerate: Shipping a profile we are unsure of to hit a Tuesday delivery.
Specificity
Definition: Every claim is anchored in something a customer can taste, see, or visit.
Behavior we expect: A bag without farm name, altitude, and process gets re-bagged.
Will not tolerate: "Smooth, balanced, complex" as a tasting note. Lazy and meaningless.
Generosity
Definition: We share — taste, knowledge, time — beyond what the transaction requires.
Behavior we expect: Give a free taste of the new arrival to every regular for the first week.
Will not tolerate: Charging a wholesale partner for the brewing recipe sheet.

11 · Brand Movement (Catalyst 17 layer)

Coffee Should Taste Like Where It's From

Enemy: The industrial coffee chain that flattens every bean into a single brown taste, where neither the drinker nor the farmer is given the dignity of a real conversation.

"Know the farm. Brew the bean. Earn the cup."

Ways to join:

  • Show up at Saturday brewing class — first one is free, all skill levels.
  • Ask the barista what farm your espresso came from — every shift can answer.
  • Subscribe to the seasonal rotation — one bag every six weeks.
  • Bring a friend who has only ever drunk supermarket coffee.
  • Use #knowthefarm when you post a cup.

How we measure growth:

  • Saturday class repeat rate (not first-timers, returners).
  • Wholesale partners who name the farm on their menu (not just "Lumen blend").
  • Subscription customers who graduate to a second farm of their choosing.
